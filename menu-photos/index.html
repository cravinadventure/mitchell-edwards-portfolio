01 — How it works

One week, start to finish.

Most menus come together over about a week of normal service. Nobody closes the kitchen, and nobody needs to know anything about photography.

  1. Day 1
    Send your menu.

    Your current menu is all it takes to start. It becomes the shot list: every dish, organized by menu section, so nothing gets missed.

  2. Days 1 to 5
    Shoot as you go.

    Whoever is on the pass, the chef or anyone on staff, snaps one phone photo of each dish before it goes out. Any phone, any lighting, straight from the kitchen. Send them in as you take them.

  3. As they come in
    We finish each photo.

    The angle and lighting are adjusted and every dish lands on the same clean background. The chef's plating is never changed. Each finished image is checked against your original photo and your menu before it's approved.

  4. Midweek
    You get the missing list.

    We send back a short list of the dishes still needed to complete the menu, plus any photo worth retaking. You shoot those during the next service or two.

  5. End of the week
    Your full menu set.

    Every dish, one consistent look, organized by menu section and sized for each delivery app, your website and social. The files are yours to use anywhere.

04 — Why the whole menu

The photo is the storefront.

On a delivery app, nobody walks past the kitchen or sees the plate at the next table. The photo is what they order from. DoorDash's own data on more than 15,000 merchants found menus with item photos get up to 44% more monthly sales.

Chains already run one consistent look across every item. Most independent restaurants have a few photos, a few gaps and a mix of styles. A full, matching menu closes that gap.

05 — Questions

Good to know.

Do you change the food?
No. The angle and lighting are adjusted. The chef's plating stays exactly as it's served, and every finished image is checked against your original photo before it's delivered.
What if our photos aren't very good?
That's expected. Kitchen lighting and a quick phone shot are fine. If a photo can't be used, it goes on the missing list with a note on what to retake.
Who takes the photos?
Anyone on staff. It takes a few seconds per plate while the dish is on the pass.
How do we send them?
A shared folder or a text thread, whatever is easiest for the kitchen.
What about new dishes later?
Send them the same way whenever the menu changes, and they're finished to match the rest of the set.
Do you need to be local?
No. The whole service runs remotely, so it works for restaurants anywhere, including new openings that need a full menu set before launch.
