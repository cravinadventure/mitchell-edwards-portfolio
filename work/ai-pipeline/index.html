01

The problem

Every creative team loses hours to the same three bottlenecks. Someone has to sit through hours of raw footage to log the usable takes. One approved concept has to be adapted for every channel, placement, and spec on the list. Finished work then has to be captioned, sized, and trafficked out to each platform.

And around all of it, feedback and approvals live in three different places, so someone is always chasing a decision: is this approved, did that go out, can we get fifteen minutes to talk about it.

None of that is design work. It is the work that happens before, after, and around the design, and it is where the week goes. So I set those jobs up to run on their own, and the hours go back into the part a client actually pays for.

02

The three steps

Editing. I drop in the raw footage and walk away. It comes back transcribed word for word with timestamps, sorted into chapters, and built into a rough cut that opens directly in Premiere Pro with markers already placed.

A big batch of video can take hours to process, but it is machine time, not a person's time, and I can run several shoots at once while I work on something else or sleep. The part that used to be a person watching footage and typing notes is simply not a job anymore.

Everything also stays logged and organized permanently. Footage from a shoot last year is searchable by what was said in it, so pulling clips for a new edit takes seconds instead of an afternoon of scrubbing through drives. The library gets more useful the longer it runs.

Design. I build the approved concept once and turn it into a template. Every adaptation the campaign needs, each channel, placement, and spec, gets generated from that same brand file. Nobody rebuilds a layout by hand, and nothing drifts off spec between versions.

Publishing. Finished pieces get captions written, files built to each platform spec, and posts scheduled across accounts automatically, so nothing sits finished and unposted waiting on someone with a free afternoon. In a previous in-house role I set up this same kind of automation and it cut scheduling time by about 75%.

03

And one layer that runs across all three

The three steps above move the work forward. This part keeps everyone around it in the loop, which in my experience is where most projects actually stall.

Coordination. Every decision and delivery gets written to a shared log the moment it happens, so the update is already there before anyone thinks to ask for one. Nobody has to chase a status, and I never have to stop working to write one.

It is kept in plain files that are easy for people to read, and easy for any AI tools your team already uses to read as well, so nothing has to be re-explained. Check-ins get put on the calendar automatically instead of sitting in a thread for two days. After work goes live, performance numbers come back in, so the next round is built on what actually worked instead of on a guess.

The effect is fewer status meetings, because the status is already written down before the meeting would have been called, and faster decisions, because the person who needs to weigh in gets asked at the moment it matters.

04

What this means if you hire me

Most designers use these tools. I set up the systems around them, and that setup comes with me when I join a team.

In practice that means faster turnarounds without adding headcount, brand consistency enforced by templates instead of by meetings, and a content calendar that stays full because the repetitive parts run themselves. I build these tools around the software your team already uses, so it fits into your workflow instead of replacing it.

05

It runs the client side too

The same approach handles intake and files. A new client fills out a questionnaire and the system builds their folders, records, and brief in about a minute. Every file that arrives gets named and filed automatically. A running log records each decision and delivery.

The case studies on this site were written straight from those logs. That is why they can walk through a 96-hour project hour by hour.
