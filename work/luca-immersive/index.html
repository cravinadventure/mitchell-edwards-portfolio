Case study · Interactive live event

The crowd became the visuals.

I built a live show where the audience drew with light and the projections answered instantly. West End Johnnie's Underground, Boston, February 1, 2025, with Pirone Productions.

01 — The brief

Make the room part of the show.

LUCA IMMERSIVE makes multi-sensory live experiences led by the character LUCA, mixing visual storytelling, music, and interactive technology. For this event the goal was simple to say and hard to build: the audience should not watch the visuals, they should make them.

The promo: the world built before the doors opened

03 — The world

Where the character comes from.

A character alone is a costume. The world is what makes it a story, so I built the place LUCA moves through: the light, the landscape, and the mood the live show would later borrow from.

Designing the world first meant the event had a source to pull from. The projections, the posters, and the room all came from the same visual language instead of being matched up after the fact.

04 — Before the doors opened

The build was the show before the show.

For the month before the event, the build itself became the content. Vlogs, hype edits, and collabs built the world online before anyone walked through the door.

The ticket page needed the same treatment. Posh data showed that around 20% of ticket buyers found the event by scrolling the main Posh feed, and that 90 to 95% of the event posters in that feed were static images. In a feed of still posters, motion was the fastest way to catch the eye.

So I rebuilt the poster as an animated stage preview: lasers, lights, and motion hinting at what the night could become. It became the page background, and because that animation also plays inside every shared link preview, every text someone sent moved too.

The animated poster: motion in a feed of still images
05 — The live loop

We handed the visuals to the room.

The whole show ran on a four-step loop with no delay in it. What the crowd did with their hands appeared on the building a moment later.

01 · The crowd held the tools

Attendees drew with light using laser pointers and flashlights attached to their LUCA IMMERSIVE wristbands.

02 · A live feed caught it

A GoPro streamed the room into the computer. The eye of the whole system.

03 · Effects added live

I layered effects in real time in Resolume: RGB splits and delay, turning the light into colorful tracers and echoes.

04 · Projected instantly

Projection mapped onto the front facade and a background screen. The room watched itself become the art.

Play nobody scripted
Visuals driven live from the room

Something happens when an experience is truly immersive. It sparks play and collaboration you cannot predict. The visuals stopped being a backdrop and became something people made together.

06 — The result

A full production, not just a night.

Journey of Creation was captured, mixed, marketed, and sold end to end: multicam capture from two angles plus a screen recording of the visuals, cut into content afterward.

50% of all profits were donated to the California Fire Foundation.

What it did for the business

The interactive format was the marketing. Because the show made its own eye-catching content, promotion cost nothing beyond the design, and one in five ticket buyers arrived straight from that content in the feed.
