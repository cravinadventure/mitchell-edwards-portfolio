Case study · Product design and build

A camera app in a band's own style.

I designed and built a small video app for TWINEM, a twin DJ duo. Point a phone at anything and it comes back looking like their visuals. It is live, it is theirs, and it sits in their Instagram bio.

Designed and coded by meRuns in any browserTheir brand, their song
This is the real app. Turn the knobs, switch the look, load a video or use your camera
01 — Why it exists

The tool came before the idea.

Making TWINEM's visual pack, I needed a specific pixelated look. The plugins that do it well were too expensive for the timeline, so I wrote my own tool in about an hour. That tool became the signature look of the whole pack.

When the pack shipped, the tool was still sitting there. So I put a real interface on it, painted it in their brand, and handed it over. A pack of files eventually gets old. An app that makes more of them does not.

02 — The design

A television, not a control panel.

The first version looked like software: sliders, labels, a preview window. Correct, but nobody wants to play with a settings menu. Their whole aesthetic is Y2K, so I rebuilt it as an old TV set. Knobs you drag, chunky buttons, a glass screen.

That changed how people use it. A slider says adjust a parameter. A knob says turn me. Every lit pixel on the screen is drawn with their four-point star instead of a square, so the picture is made out of their logo.

03 — What it does

Everything a fan needs, nothing else.

Load a video or turn on the camera. Four knobs control the look. Two buttons switch between the star-pixel style and the TV-line style, both taken straight from the visuals I made for their show. Three keys hold their brand colors.

Their song plays while you use it and the picture pulses to the beat, because the app listens to the music live. Hit record and it saves a video of the screen with their track already in it. Whatever a fan makes and posts carries their look and their music.

Their logo running through the app, the channel it opens on
04 — The result

They put it in their bio.

The duo linked it in their Instagram bio the day they got it. Every fan who opens it makes something that looks like TWINEM and sounds like TWINEM.

What it did for the business

A visual pack gets used for one show. This turned the same style into something the duo can use forever, and something their fans use for them. It costs nothing to run and keeps producing branded content long after the event it was made for.
